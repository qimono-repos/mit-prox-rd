## Reply (ready to paste)

The qualification is fair for today's hardware. But a low qubit count is also a good starting point: engineering is the science of managing scarcity. Two or three qubits are enough to watch superposition, interference and basic algorithms, like the ones we study in this course. Combined with hybrid classical-quantum approaches (for example, quantum machine learning), they are already a level up from a simulator.

That is why, in my country, Argentina, universities started small. [UNAHUR](https://unahur.edu.ar/) (University National of the [City Hurlingham](https://maps.app.goo.gl/ytEUcYR2fxPXumrM8), in Buenos Aires Province) installed a 3-qubit NMR [SpinQ](https://www.spinq.com/en/), the first quantum computer in Argentina's public university system, and leads the [Iniciativa Cuántica](https://unahur.edu.ar/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Declaracion-de-Intencion-para-la-promocion-y-desarrollo-de-una-iniciativa-cuantica.pdf) with UNICEN (University National of the Center of the Province of Buenos Aires), UNRN (University National of Río Negro), UNLP (University National of La Plata) and others ([press coverage](https://www.lagaceta.com.ar/nota/1117347/sociedad/universidad-publica-suma-computacion-cuantica-para-sirve-clave.html)).

For a startup like Qimono (TM), where I do my freelance work, I asked SpinQ directly for a quote on machines for local development, and they quoted me USD 12,000 for a 2-qubit machine, about the size of a large briefcase. Small machines lower the entry barrier.

On measurable advantage and qubit count: Google's [Willow](https://blog.google/technology/research/google-willow-quantum-chip/) went below the error-correction threshold, so the real race is gate depth at low logical error, not the number of qubits. The same applies to Microsoft's [Majorana 2](https://news.microsoft.com/source/features/innovation/majorana-2-microsoft-discovery-agentic-ai/): about 20 seconds of parity lifetime is impressive, but parity lifetime is not coherence.

That is how these topics connect to Dario Gil: this is the path to the potential he described, and the people who will walk it are being trained on small machines today. I believe it will grow with each iteration, just as the Amazon professional explained in one of the course's industry tour videos: it took a decade of machine learning before ChatGPT hit.
