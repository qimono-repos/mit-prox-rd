# Argentina's science budget: a long, uneven curve

This folder documents a historical comparison relevant to the Argentina case in the quantum-technology essay.

## Files

- `argentina-science-budget.svg`: vector chart, suitable for embedding in Markdown or a document.
- `argentina-science-budget.csv`: the exact plotted points and their status/source notes.

## What the chart measures

The series is the **National Budget Function for Science and Technology (Función Ciencia y Técnica, FCyT) as a percentage of GDP**. It is not the same thing as total national R&D expenditure, CONICET's individual budget, or private-sector R&D.

## Important limitation

The plotted series uses published historical marker values from the Grupo EPC–ANPRES report, rather than a complete year-by-year table. The line segments are visual guides between selected observations; they must not be read as measured annual values. The 2026 point is a projection based on budget execution through September 2026, not a final audited full-year result.

The Grupo EPC–ANPRES December 2025 report gives 0.167% of GDP for 2025, below the 0.177% reported for 2002. CIICTI's September 2026 report estimates 0.164% for 2026, which it describes as the lowest level in the series beginning in 1972.

## Essay use

A defensible summary is: Argentina's public science-and-technology budget has fluctuated over more than five decades, with notable recoveries as well as contractions; the latest reported years place it at the bottom of the historical series. This gives context to the contrast between Argentina's ongoing quantum research projects and the continuity required to sustain advanced research infrastructure.
